# Culture and people recommendations

> **Read this first.** Austin has been inside Aperitif for about two and a half weeks. These are observations from a management-consulting lens, to be taken with a grain of salt. They come from short interviews, not from living in the business. Joe and Julia know the people and the history far better. Treat each one as a question worth discussing, not a verdict.

**What this section is.** Each point below is a pattern in how people work together, not a tool fault. None of them is fixed by a build. Each one has:
- **What we saw** – the evidence and how many people raised it ("raised by" counts people who said it themselves; Austin's prompts don't count).
- **Why it matters.**
- **The recommendation** – a people or process change.
- **Solutions that support it** – DS items that help, but don't replace the people change.

**Where the evidence comes from.** The Stage 4 culture sweep (8 Nov) re-read all 14 transcripts and logged 153 culture items in 15 themes. Six of these became problems P-43 to P-48. P-41 (thin management layer) and P-21 (managers at capacity) come from the main clustering; both are covered here because they can't be solved by a build.

**Decisions on these problems** (for transparency): P-44, P-47 and P-48 are "Solve now", with DS-02 or DS-15 named as the fix. P-43, P-45 and P-46 were not taken into the solutions. P-41 is not in this proposal as a build – it's a culture issue, covered here. P-21 stays "Solve now" but is handled here, not by a build. All eight are included so Joe sees the full picture.

## 1. Managers are at capacity – Tier 2/3 clients get dropped first (P-21)

**What we saw**
- One of the two most widely raised problems in the audit (Stage 1 calls it the widest raise): **7 staff + Joe and Julia, 15 pain points** (C-17). Only AI use being personal and ungoverned (P-40) is raised as widely. Staff: Shrishti Dokwal, Laura Shillington, Diya Manoj, Michaela Cruz, Sophie Hesseborn, Sid Bali, Wendlley Simoes.
- Loads of 12–20 clients per manager, plus ad hoc work, with no capacity model.
- *"the first set of clients that take a hit in performance or get neglected are those tier 3, tier 2 clients"* – Shrishti Dokwal, Senior Performance Marketing Manager (SD L110)
- Wendlley's own channel load on four clients nearly doubled since July (about 10 to 18 channels) with no extra resource (WS-PP01). SQR hygiene and Merchant Center checks slip (SD-PP08, MC-PP18).
- Overload is absorbed quietly – 11 staff (culture theme CP-08): *"I was working out of hours a bit just to get it all done because there was just too much going on."* – Karl Zaharopoulos (KZ L201). *"but it's like kind of that's just what it is"* – Sarah Julian (SJ L163).

**Why it matters**
- Tier 2/3 clients are most of the book. Quiet neglect there is where churn starts.
- People absorbing load personally is a burnout and resignation risk. One recent resignation was linked partly to workload – Laura said salary was also a factor (culture item A-K21).

**Recommendation**
- **Agree a simple load rule per role.** Use the hours already on each client's monthly Monday tickets to compare planned vs real effort – no timesheets.
- **Decide what Tier 2/3 always gets.** A short minimum-service list, so a drop in attention is a choice, not an accident.
- **Cap overlapping onboardings** per manager, and send access requests with the contract.
- **Set hiring triggers** from the load rule, so a hire starts before people are stretched (a new starter takes about 90 days to ramp – KO-PP09).
- These are management choices for Joe and Julia, not builds.

**Solutions that support it** (they reduce effort; they don't fix capacity)
- DS-01 pacing and alerts, DS-29 PPC Monday check – managers open an account when flagged, not every day.
- DS-06 request sweeper and DS-30 daily priorities – fewer lost requests; the right work surfaces first.
- DS-05 deck generator (partly) – smaller-client 6-month reviews take less time (LS-PP02).
- DS-28 client comms assistant – comms stop crowding out strategy (SH-PP09).

## 2. The management layer is thin (P-41)

**What we saw**
- Raised by **Wendlley + Joe and Julia** (C-37). Mostly a leadership view.
- *"At the moment, we're a bit headless when it comes to performance. We really want a performance — more senior leader."* – Julia (KO L066)
- The Head of Performance left about two weeks before Wendlley's interview. Wendlley absorbed a Tier 1 account they hadn't worked on (WS-PP11).
- Leads were promoted from within without people-leadership training: *"they've only been in management since they've been with us."* – Joe (MP L465). Joe also said leads don't have the soft skills yet to take escalations (MP-PP08).
- Note: who leads performance today was described differently in different interviews (contradiction X-09).

**Why it matters**
- Decisions and sign-offs flow up to a few people (Julia, Wendlley, Joe). That is the bottleneck the escalation rules in DS-14 Part B try to fix – and those rules only work if leads are ready to act.

**Recommendation**
- **Decide the senior performance seat:** hire, appoint an interim, or formally split the role. Write down who leads performance so the team hears one answer.
- **Give every lead short, practical people-leadership coaching:** difficult conversations, escalations, giving feedback. Start with the leads who'll take escalations first.
- **Pair each new lead with a senior for their first three months of escalations.**

**Solutions that support it**
- DS-14 Part B (escalation and delegation rules, from DS-13) – says who acts on what.
- DS-10 systems register – less reliance on one senior person to explain how things work.

## 3. A calm, loyal culture makes standards hard to enforce (P-43)

**What we saw**
- Raised by **Karl, Tonie, Wendlley + Joe and Julia** (Stage 4, CP-01).
- Leadership worries that pushing performance will make people leave: *"But now with this next generation, they just quit, man."* – Joe (MP L313)
- Long relationships make firmer expectations feel personal (MP L330). After repeated lapses on pacing sheets, Joe's fallback was a written-warning, three-strikes rule (MP L379).
- Performance is often judged by feel: *"I have a sense feel as well, like of how the person is performing."* – Wendlley (WS L413)
- Peers avoid raising gaps: *"I don't want to throw the tech tape under the bus."* – Karl (KZ L316)
- This is the shadow side of strength 1 (a calm, peaceful culture).

**Why it matters**
- When standards rest on relationships, reminders replace accountability. Joe: *"you can build all the best processes in the world but nobody actually follows them."* (MP L229)
- Every new system in this pack will face the same test.

**Recommendation**
- **Make expectations impersonal.** A handful of visible standards per role (for example, "every live channel has a pacing line", "tasks updated by Friday"), owned by team leads – not timesheets.
- **Let the system show the gap, not the founder.** Where a solution produces a status (pacing coverage, overdue tasks), leads review it weekly with their team.
- **Give leads the job of raising gaps**, with a simple script, so it isn't "throwing someone under the bus".
- Keep the calm. The aim is clear standards plus calm, not pressure.

**Solutions that support it**
- DS-01 (pacing line coverage), DS-06 and DS-30 (task status), DS-10 (named owners) – they make standards visible without anyone chasing.

## 4. Change fatigue and quiet AI anxiety slow adoption (P-44)

**What we saw**
- Raised by **Hayley Tantau, Sophie Hesseborn + Joe** (Stage 4, CP-03).
- Three systems arrived in about 50 days – Monday.com, AgencyAnalytics and Employment Hero. Staff survey feedback: *"too many changes in a short amount of time."* (Joe, KO L381)
- Joe: *"I think there's probably a bit of instilled fear in there as well."* (KO L412). A writer worries about using AI *"without making myself obsolete."* – Hayley (HT L292)
- Tools that exist aren't used: Joe's AI assistant in Slack – *"No one utilises it."* (KO L377). Meeting note-takers were tried, but notes don't become actions (SH L396).

**Why it matters**
- This pack proposes a lot of change. Without a plan for adoption, it will land the same way the last three tools did.

**Recommendation**
- **Say clearly, and often, what AI is for:** to take the grind out of the work, not to replace people. Austin opened interviews with this message (*"my job is not to automate anyone's job. It's to augment"* – LS L002). Joe and Julia saying it carries more weight.
- **Stage the rollout.** One change per team at a time, with a pause before the next. Don't launch everything in the first 90 days.
- **Protect learning time** – for example, one hour a week per person in the first month of each new tool.
- **Name one AI champion per team**, from the people already building their own tools (strength 15).
- **Talk to the writers directly** about human writing as Aperitif's point of difference, and where AI helps them rather than replaces them.

**Solutions that support it**
- DS-02 AI playbook (Austin's named fix) – one clear set of rules and shared tools instead of tools appearing ad hoc.
- DS-15 AI Business Brain – lives in Slack, where people already work; no new app to learn.

## 5. Ideas and concerns are held back (P-45)

**What we saw**
- Raised by **Diya Manoj, Zach Chamberlain + Julia** (Stage 4, CP-05).
- *"someone did say to me that when Joe or myself are on a call, they feel like they automatically stuff up what they're saying."* – Julia (KO L336). She put it down to respect for Joe as founder (KO L334).
- *"Like wild ideas, I would say, because I'm not sure like how much is the possibility of putting that into action."* – Diya (DM L129)
- Ideas raised with a lead have no clear route: *"It's hard to kickstart that and know where to go."* – Zach (ZC L494)

**Why it matters**
- Staff have plenty of ideas (strength 5 – the widest positive theme). Without a route, they stall, and problems get treated as normal.

**Recommendation**
- **Give ideas a home and an owner.** One channel or a standing 15 minutes in a monthly meeting, with a named person who replies to every idea within two weeks – yes, no or not yet.
- **Let juniors lead part of client calls and WIPs**, with leaders speaking last.
- **Close the loop in public.** When a staff idea gets used, say whose it was.

**Solutions that support it**
- DS-02 AI playbook – a place where staff-built prompts and skills are shared and credited.

## 6. Client-pleasing comes before margin, scope and staff time (P-46)

**What we saw**
- Raised by **7 staff, no leadership**: Hayley, Karl, Laura, Shrishti, Sophie, Wendlley, Zach (Stage 4, CP-09).
- *"And they pay so much, we kind of just have to do this."* – Karl, on a bespoke manual report (KZ L427). *"I'll drop everything and do it."* (KZ L474)
- New clients are over-serviced in month one (ZC L123). Work started for a client who still had no website two months in (WS L710).
- *"Based on the 24-hour turnaround time, I would have to focus on the comms"* – Sophie (SH L334)
- Upselling is rare: *"[Karl]'s done a few upsells, but not much. That's an opportunity."* – Laura (LS L076)
- The shadow side of strength 8 (retention as the shared goal).

**Why it matters**
- Retention is watched closely; margin per client isn't. Staff time leaks into work nobody is paid for.

**Recommendation**
- **Write a simple service boundary per tier:** what's included, what's extra, and the standard words for "that's extra – here's what it would cost".
- **Review margin per client alongside retention** in the monthly retention review, at least for the top 10 clients.
- **Make growth part of the account manager's job**, with short coaching in WIPs, so an upsell is advice, not hard selling (strength 9).

**Solutions that support it**
- DS-14 growth signals – spots room to expand from what clients say.
- DS-28 client comms assistant – drafts updates that state the problem and a recommendation.
- DS-05 – the price calculator in every proposal; DS-08 – delivery confirms scope at handover.

## 7. Teams work in silos (P-47)

**What we saw**
- Raised by **Diya, Hayley, Karl, Laura + Julia** (Stage 4, CP-12).
- *"I find now it almost feels outsourced in a way where it's just permanently handled."* – Karl, on technical SEO (KZ L307)
- *"people are quite — can be a bit channel-blind, I think, because we're so specialised"* – Julia (KO L289)
- *"We all have a pretty basic understanding. There's definitely room for some cross-team knowledge sharing."* – Laura (LS L058)
- Managers don't know how specialists' work gets done (DM L642). Content and SEO only aligned on brief quality after a joint training (HT L072).

**Why it matters**
- Clients buy one agency. Silos mean missed cross-channel ideas and technical work that slips months (P-35, not in this proposal).

**Recommendation**
- **A 30-minute monthly priority check between each strategist and the technical team** – agree the month's tasks by impact. (It's a process change, not a build, so it's offered here as a habit.)
- **A short cross-team show-and-tell each month** – one team shows how a piece of their work is made.
- **Repeat what worked:** the joint content–SEO brief training fixed a real problem (HT L072).

**Solutions that support it**
- DS-15 AI Business Brain (Austin's named fix) and DS-04 client brains – every team reads the same picture of each client.

## 8. Problems are fixed after they break (P-48)

**What we saw**
- Raised by **Diya, Karl, Sophie, Sarah, Wendlley + Joe** (Stage 4, CP-15).
- *"I've just been making do because we haven't been able to figure out a fix for this."* – Sarah (SJ L292)
- After the LinkedIn gap: *"Like, okay, I have to do it now."* – Joe (MP L577)
- Quality depends on individual vigilance: *"you just have to be like super into the nitty gritty"* – Sophie, on Meta's auto-on settings (SH L177). *"Technically, you should probably look over it with a fine tooth comb."* – Karl (KZ L539)
- When the Meta data feed broke, Wendlley worked around it personally with Claude (WS second session, L434).

**Why it matters**
- Fixes depend on someone noticing. When that person is busy or away, things break quietly (the pacing feed, the reminder bot).

**Recommendation**
- **Name an owner for every tool and every recurring check** – designated champions who are capable of keeping it running.
- **Set aside a little time each month for root-cause fixes** – the workarounds people have "been making do" with.
- **Add "who owns it and what to do if it breaks" to the definition of done** for anything new.

**Solutions that support it**
- DS-15 AI Business Brain (Austin's named fix), with the early-warning solutions under it: DS-01 pacing alerts, DS-07 link watchdog, DS-14 health signals, DS-16 content radar.
- DS-10 systems register – every build has an owner and a break-fix runbook.
