# What Aperitif does well

Before the problems, here is what's working. These are the things to protect while anything changes.

**How this list was built.** A second pass over all 14 interview transcripts looked only for what's good (488 positive items, grouped into 20 themes – Stage 4 culture and strengths sweep, 8 Nov). Two points are Austin's own observations, added at his request (S-14, S-15). "Raised by" counts people who said it themselves; things Austin said and someone agreed with aren't counted. Theme IDs (ST-xx, S-xx) trace back to the research in Notion.

---

## Culture and people

### 1. A calm, peaceful place people want to work
- **What we saw:** a relaxed, good-humoured tone, people who enjoy the job, and several who say their load is manageable right now. Austin's own impression after 2.5 weeks inside: the culture is very peaceful (S-15).
- **Raised by:** 11 staff + Joe (ST-06).
- **In their words:**
  - *"There's always something, so there's no point in getting worked up about it when it's just another day in agency there."* – Joe Romeo (mid-point, MP L569)
  - *"it is a good job. I enjoy it."* – Tonie Guevarra, Outreach Specialist (TG L371)
- **Watch-out:** the same calm can make standards hard to enforce (see culture recommendations, P-43).

### 2. Trust and autonomy – judged on output, not hours
- **What we saw:** people run their own month and are judged on what they deliver. Leadership has chosen not to use timesheets.
- **Raised by:** 6 staff + Joe and Julia (ST-01).
- **In their words:**
  - *"you're pretty much like given free reign to like finish all of your work by the end of..."* – Sarah Julian, Content Manager (SJ L044)
  - On timesheets: *"I think it corrodes cultures."* – Julia Van Der Linden, Head of Client Services (KO L209)

### 3. Mistakes are handled without blame
- **What we saw:** errors are met calmly. People own their part and feel able to say when they're struggling.
- **Raised by:** 11 staff + Joe (ST-02, S-07).
- **In their words:**
  - *"we just said, 'hey, it could happen to anybody.'"* – Joe Romeo (KO L339)
  - *"I was yesterday like telling my manager like not like a complaint but I was just you know feeling bad"* – Diya Manoj, Performance Marketing Manager (DM L440)

### 4. People help each other
- **What we saw:** colleagues pitch in at peaks and during leave, and work across SEO, technical, content and paid.
- **Raised by:** 10 staff (ST-07).
- **In their words:**
  - *"The team helped me with like the keyword research and mapping and stuff like that."* – Zach Chamberlain, SEO Manager (ZC L218)
  - *"I need like an expert opinion or something. That is when like when we, you know, jumps in and help me"* – Diya Manoj (DM L072)

### 5. Ideas come from the floor
- **What we saw:** staff turned up to interviews with their own idea lists, research fixes themselves and pick up new tools quickly. Zach wrote his ideas up in a document ("AI suggestions", cited in DS-04, DS-07, DS-12, DS-16, DS-22 to DS-24).
- **Raised by:** 13 staff + Joe and Julia – the widest positive theme (ST-04, S-13).
- **In their words:**
  - *"I've written a few notes down about the kind of like suggestions and stuff"* – Zach Chamberlain (ZC L037)
  - *"I also Googled asking like if we can integrate those things on Monday"* – Diya Manoj (DM L158)

### 6. People are grown from within
- **What we saw:** leads are promoted from inside and coached. One-on-ones, development matrices, training plans and weekly training sessions are in place.
- **Raised by:** 10 staff + Joe and Julia (ST-08, S-09).
- **In their words:**
  - *"We do weekly one-on-ones, use training plans and progression plans and all that kind of stuff as well."* – Karl Zaharopoulos, Senior SEO Manager & Link-building Lead (KZ L089)
  - *"They've been all promoted into those roles. So We're taking them on the journey of helping filling in those blanks."* – Joe Romeo (MP L465)

### 7. Pride in the work, and human writing as a point of difference
- **What we saw:** people care how the work looks. Hand-written content is a stated value; writers keep their own clients and learn from client edits.
- **Raised by:** pride – 8 staff + Joe (ST-09); human writing – 4 staff + Julia (ST-11, S-11).
- **In their words:**
  - *"I think there's a lot of pride. A lot of pride."* – Joe Romeo (KO L097)
  - *"the greatest point of difference is now being able human like adding that human touch again"* – Hayley Tantau, Content Specialist (HT L349)
  - *"We obviously have a strong brand, and our writers are amazing."* – Laura Shillington, SEO Director (LS L036)

## Clients

### 8. Proactive, honest client communication – and retention as the shared goal
- **What we saw:** fast replies, plain-English updates, mid-month warnings and ideas brought to clients unprompted. Everyone names retention as the goal, and many clients are long-standing.
- **Raised by:** communication – 9 staff + Joe (ST-13, S-06); retention – 7 staff + Joe and Julia (ST-14).
- **In their words:**
  - *"we want to be on the offence, not on the defence, when it comes to explaining what results are."* – Joe Romeo (KO L354)
  - *"this is what's happened. This is what I'm seeing. This is what I recommend."* – Shrishti Dokwal, Senior Performance Marketing Manager (SD L138)
  - *"I think everyone's goals are like retention, basically."* – Wendlley Simoes, Performance Marketing Director (WS L407)
- **Watch-out:** client-pleasing can beat margin and scope (P-46).

### 9. Advice in the client's interest
- **What we saw:** budgets are run for the client's return, platform upsells are judged against strategy, and growth comes from results rather than hard selling.
- **Raised by:** 5 staff + Julia (ST-12).
- **In their words:**
  - *"not just like putting more budget towards brand, if it's like if it doesn't make sense, etc. because it's very easy to do that."* – Wendlley Simoes (WS L138)
  - *"people have burnt their relationships with clients by trying to upsell them."* – Julia, on other agencies (KO L294)

## Quality and ways of working

### 10. A person checks the work – including anything AI touches
- **What we saw:** seniors review strategies, builds and copy, and explain why. AI output is always checked by a person.
- **Raised by:** 11 staff (ST-10, S-07).
- **In their words:**
  - *"It's a good process to send it back to the writer so they can see for next time what changes that they want."* – Zach Chamberlain (ZC L446)
  - *"we need to go over it. We need to like double check it all and make sure it makes sense"* – Karl Zaharopoulos, on keyword research done with Claude and Ahrefs (KZ L182)

### 11. A predictable month and good written standards where they exist
- **What we saw:** a shared monthly cycle (briefs week 1, writing weeks 1–3, QA last week), Tier 1 protected under load, and brief standards that are workshopped and reviewed.
- **Raised by:** rhythm – 12 staff (ST-15, S-05); written standards – 12 staff + Joe and Julia (ST-16, S-04).
- **In their words:**
  - *"It's pretty predictable, which is really, really nice."* – Hayley Tantau (HT L159)
  - *"Every sort of six months or so we like workshop the content briefing process."* – Sarah Julian (SJ L124)
  - *"the briefs have been incredible since then."* – Hayley Tantau (HT L098)

### 12. Commercial discipline at the top
- **What we saw:** monthly financials within days, DLER used as a utilisation check, retention tracked automatically, and calculators that tie retainers to hours.
- **Raised by:** 6 staff + Joe and Julia (ST-19, S-08, S-10).
- **In their words:**
  - *"We have a calculator that works out how many words, links and hours make up the monthly retainer."* – Laura Shillington (LS L022)
  - *"we can't be promising all of these things and including them in the scope when like it's not even going to be profitable at that level."* – Joe Romeo (MP L165)

## Tools

### 13. A lean, clean set-up – including Zapier
- **What we saw:** a deliberately small stack: HubSpot for sales, Monday.com for delivery, Make and Zapier as the glue, AgencyAnalytics for reporting. Tools are cost-checked at renewal. Austin's observation: the Zapier set-up is really clean and lean, with no over-reliance on niche systems (S-14).
- **Raised by:** 13 staff + Joe and Julia (ST-17, S-03).
- **In their words:**
  - *"the moment a client gets onboarded and a deal is signed on HubSpot, all the onboarding tasks go into Monday."* – Sid Bali, Technical SEO Specialist (SB L094)
  - *"I'm not asking to go ahead with Ruby as well because I know that Monday involves another cost"* – Diya Manoj (DM L158)
- **Note:** in interviews the HubSpot-to-Monday glue is Make; Zapier runs Slack automations and announcements (context pack). The Monday/Make set-up has one builder, Sid (see implementation risks).

### 14. Reporting is already automated
- **What we saw:** AgencyAnalytics builds reports and gives clients self-serve dashboards, with little re-keying from GA4 now.
- **Raised by:** 8 sources across leadership, SEO, paid and technical SEO (S-01).
- **Why it matters:** this is why monthly reports stay in AgencyAnalytics and aren't rebuilt (DS-05 covers review decks only).

### 15. Home-grown AI tools already save real time
- **What we saw:** people have built their own Claude and Gemini tools that work. Karl's Claude SEO-strategy prompt guide cut onboarding strategy from about 3 days to roughly an hour (S-02; the context pack gives 75–90 min). Wendlley's Gemini forecasting takes about 15 minutes.
- **Raised by:** 11 staff + Joe (ST-18, S-02).
- **In their words:**
  - *"I used to take like hours doing this and Gemini did it in like 15 minutes and gave me scenarios"* – Wendlley Simoes (WS L462)
- **Watch-out:** most of these live on personal accounts. DS-02 moves them into a shared, governed library.

## What this means for the recommendations

- **Keep the calm and the trust.** Nothing here proposes timesheets or staff tracking (DS-15 rules it out).
- **Build on what's there** – Monday, AgencyAnalytics, Canva and the team's own AI tools – before adding new systems.
- **Use the people who already have ideas** as early testers and champions.
