## Stage 3 – Findings narrative

> **Stage 3 findings (6 Nov).** Built from Stage 1 (39 clusters, 8 root causes, 31 contradictions) and Stage 2 (34 process cost rows, 35 merged opportunities). Every paragraph cites its IDs: RC = root cause, C = cluster, O = merged opportunity, X = contradiction, S = strength; extraction IDs (e.g. SD-PP04) can be found with Ctrl-F in the interview files. Raise counts are "N staff + N leadership" and count only interviewee or detected sources; interviewer-prompted agreement is corroboration only. Hours are monthly, at $80/hr, and carry their certainty. Sections 1–8 use role families; the names behind them and the dependency names are in section 9.

**Bottom line.** Aperitif's time and margin leak through eight shared causes, not 39 separate problems. The biggest is that there is no system of record: budgets, client context, briefs, links and files live in hand-kept copies, so skilled people re-key and collate (RC-01, 12 staff + 2 leadership). The clearest cash loss traces to human monitoring: the one stated loss, a $10K overspend credit (MP-PP01, stated), came from pacing that ran against the wrong figure and a channel that wasn't paced at all (RC-02, C-01, C-03). The work the interviewees described adds up to 944–1,901 hours a month ($75K–$152K), but only 12.8% of the mid-point hours rest on a time someone actually stated, so the figures show which processes are large, not a precise saving. Eighteen of the 35 fixes are quick wins that need no build, led by restoring the pacing feed and covering every live channel (O-01), a written AI-data rule with company seats and a shared skills library (O-02), and AI-drafted briefs from the plan that already exists (O-03).

## 1. Headline findings

Eight shared root causes (RC-01 to RC-08) drive the 39 clusters. For each: what it is, how well it is evidenced, what it costs, and which fix leads. **How to read the cost lines.** "Recoverable" is the sum of the Stage 2 hours-saved ranges for the opportunities whose main cluster sits under that cause. Each opportunity is counted once, against one cause. All of these hours are *assumed* (reduction percentages applied to mostly assumed process hours). They are ranges, not targets, and the eight together (142–486 h, $11.3K–$38.9K a month) overstate the total, because several fixes draw on the same process hours (Stage 2 note).

### RC-01 · No system of record, so skilled people re-key and collate by hand

Budgets, client data, briefs, links and files are kept in several hand-maintained copies, so people re-key, reconcile and collate them. Paid budgets are typed into three or more places, so pacing runs against out-of-date figures (C-01; SH-PP01, SD-PP02, X-17). Managers spend an hour or more collating data for each half-hour client call (C-05; SD-PP05, stated per call). Reports and review decks are assembled by hand every cycle (C-06). Briefs are re-cut from a plan that already exists (C-20). Content hand-offs run on copied sheets (C-21). Every link order and invoice is re-keyed (C-23). Files and logins sit away from the work (C-34). **Evidence:** 12 staff + 2 leadership across 7 clusters, 6 of them High confidence; maximum severity 5 (MP-PP01). **Cost:** recoverable 59–176 h a month ($4.7K–$14.1K, assumed). It sits inside the largest process rows: client meetings and prep (105–188 h, 39% stated), content briefing (48–114 h) and link sourcing and ordering (43–136 h). **Fix:** quick wins O-03 (brief drafts from the Master SEO Document) and O-05 (report and review decks filled from data, narrative drafted by AI), then O-15 (enter budgets once). The builds are O-11 (pacing tracker that reads budgets), O-13 (per-client AI summary) and O-18 (one link record). O-25 and O-30 cover content intake and file storage.

### RC-02 · Monitoring is human, so problems surface late

Nothing pushes a signal on spend, performance, tracking, link status, trends or churn. Problems surface when someone happens to look. Channels beyond Google and Meta have no pacing (C-03; WS-PP02, MP-PP02). Performance drops and tracking breaks are found in manual checks, sometimes about 7 days late (C-04; SH-PP11, stated). Paid links vanish after payment and nobody re-checks them: about 100 in about 10 months (C-25; KZ-PP10, stated count). Seasonal peaks are reached too late (C-15; ZC-PP09). Churn is spotted late (C-16). One narrow alert exists, a Google Ads script that flags 7 days without conversions (X-22). **Evidence:** 9 staff + 2 leadership across 5 clusters, 4 High; maximum severity 5. C-04 alone has 7 staff raises. **Cost:** recoverable 19–54 h a month ($1.5K–$4.3K, assumed). On top of that is the avoided loss on dead links of about $800–$1,300 a month (KZ-PP10 count × context-pack unit cost, estimated) and the one $10K credit already paid (MP-PP01, stated; whether it was the untracked-channel incident is open, X-10). **Fix:** O-01 (restore the pacing feed and add every live channel) and O-10 (switch on the native platform alert rules now) this month. O-07 (automated live-link checks with publisher replacement claims) this quarter. O-12 (cross-account monitor with a draft diagnosis) is the build.

### RC-03 · Client context isn't captured per client, so every hand-off loses detail

Each client's strategy, history, rules and feedback live in the owner's memory or personal notes. Every hand-off loses detail: sales to delivery, manager to writer or implementer, and cover during leave. Briefs and cover suffer (C-07). Handovers have no required pack or trigger, so new clients are re-asked questions and an offboarding was missed (C-08; SH-PP07, MC-PP14). Regulated-industry rules aren't shared, and one client rejected content for breaching its guidelines (C-12; ZC-PP11, stated). Internal task briefs arrive incomplete (C-27). Call recordings exist but are not used (X-21). A sales-to-delivery handover exists on paper but is often late or skipped (X-16). **Evidence:** 8 staff + 0 leadership across 4 clusters, 3 High; leadership corroborates (MP-PP20, KO-PP01, both interviewer-prompted). **Cost:** recoverable 7–29 h a month ($0.6K–$2.3K, assumed). The larger cost is rework and failed cover, which is not quantified. **Fix:** O-04 (one per-client record fed automatically by call summaries) and O-08 (a sales-to-delivery gate in HubSpot with required fields and an access checklist), both this month. O-25 and O-13 extend it.

### RC-04 · Tools, automations and AI are built by individuals with no owner, so they break or stay personal

The pacing connector is failing and is not being renewed, so Meta pacing is done by hand every day (C-02; SD-PP01, MC-PP06, X-01). Meta builds and QA are manual (C-28). The HubSpot–Make–Monday integration gives every new client the wrong owner and may drop tasks (C-31; SB-PP10, KO-PP11). Home-built tools have one builder and no documentation (C-32). AI use is personal and ungoverned: there is no rule on client data, and at least one person pays for their own work account (C-33; DM-PP12, MC-PP19, stated). **Evidence:** 11 staff + 1 leadership across 5 clusters, all High. C-33 is the broadest staff raise in the audit (9 staff + 1 leadership). **Cost:** recoverable 15–59 h a month ($1.2K–$4.7K, assumed). The HubSpot and Monday stack costs about $30K a year (KO-PP12, stated by leadership; no saving quoted until invoices are checked, X-31). **Fix:** O-02 (one-page AI-data rule, company Claude seats, shared skills library) and O-01 this month. O-16 (a register of every automation, a backup admin and a monthly reconciliation) is also a quick win. O-02 is a prerequisite for every AI opportunity in the shortlist.

### RC-05 · There is no effort model, so work is sold, priced and scheduled by feel

Time per client isn't measured. Scope and onboardings aren't checked against capacity, and intake isn't triaged. Clustered onboardings swamp BAU: five in one month for one manager (C-10; ZC-PP08, stated). Managers are at capacity, and lower-tier clients and account hygiene slip first (C-17, the widest raise in the set at 7 staff + 2 leadership). Same-day requests break the planned month (C-18). Margin is known only as "roughly 30%" with a utilisation proxy (C-19; KO-PP07, stated). Technical SEO runs as a separate queue (C-26). Sales commits scope that delivery can't meet profitably (C-38; WS-PP07, stated: two months' work for a client with no website). **Evidence:** 11 staff + 2 leadership across 6 clusters, all High. C-19 and C-38 rest mostly on leadership plus one staff member each, and C-38 was first raised by the interviewer (MP-PP10). **Cost:** recoverable 7–44 h a month ($0.5K–$3.5K, assumed). The figure is low because the fixes here mostly move work rather than remove it: O-19 frees 17–29 senior hours that are redistributed, not saved. The real exposure is margin and retention, which nobody has measured. **Fix:** O-17 (cap concurrent onboardings per manager) and O-22 (calculator and margin check in every proposal) this month. O-19 (effort model from ticket hours, no timesheets) and O-14 (AI-prefilled onboarding audit) this quarter or later.

### RC-06 · Decisions and quality gates sit with a few senior people

Authority isn't delegated and the management layer is thin. Escalations, sign-offs, QA and Tier 1 relationships queue on a few people, with about five churn-risk escalations a week reaching leadership (C-36; KO-PP05, stated). Content delivery runs through a single person for editing, routing and freelancer management (C-22). Link vetting queues for one approver, and links take 1–2 months to go live (C-24; LS-PP12, stated). The senior performance seat has been vacant since mid-October (C-37; WS-PP11, X-09). Growth depends on leadership-led sales and inbound leads (C-39). **Evidence:** 8 staff + 2 leadership across 5 clusters, 4 High. C-22 is Medium (two content-team raises), and C-37 and C-39 are mostly leadership evidence. **Cost:** recoverable 12–55 h a month ($1.0K–$4.4K, assumed). The bigger cost is key-person risk (section 6). **Fix:** O-21 (written escalation and sign-off thresholds by level) this month. O-20 (shared, calibrated link-vetting skill), O-26 (spread editing) and O-32 (fill the senior performance seat) this quarter.

### RC-07 · External dependencies aren't contracted, time-boxed or tracked, so delivery waits

Client access, approvals, inputs, contractors and vendors are chased by email rather than tracked against agreed dates. Onboarding stalls on access, inputs and the tracking contractor (C-09). Content waits about a week for approval (C-11; ZC-PP06, stated). Page builds loop with clients for weeks; one looped for a month (C-13; SB-PP07, stated). Paid changes wait on client sign-off while managers carry the blame (C-14). Outsourced creative and landing pages are slow and hard to brief, at about $1,500 a landing page (C-29; WS-PP14, stated). A campaign build is about 1 day of work inside 3–5 days or more elapsed (X-12). **Evidence:** 9 staff + 1 leadership across 5 clusters, all High. **Cost:** recoverable 6–24 h a month ($0.5K–$1.9K, assumed). The main cost is elapsed time: touch-to-wait runs about 1:10 or worse for content approval and 1:3 to 1:5 for paid builds (Stage 2.1). **Fix:** O-24 (approval windows written into client agreements, with logged dates and reminders), O-08 (access checklist at signing) and O-09 (self-QA checklists for implementers), all this month.

### RC-08 · Adoption isn't designed or enforced

Systems and SOPs are built and then assumed to be used. Monday.com isn't the single home for work: requests arrive by email and Slack, statuses go stale, and reminders go unacted. One person spends about an hour a day triaging email (C-30; SB-PP02, stated per day). SOPs exist but go unread, so leaders re-explain and mistakes repeat. Three tools were rolled out in 50 days (C-35; KO-PP13, stated; MP-PP03). There is a wiki; the problem is currency and use, not absence (X-15). **Evidence:** 5 staff + 2 leadership across 2 clusters, both High. **Cost:** recoverable 17–47 h a month ($1.4K–$3.7K, assumed). **Fix:** O-06 (all internal requests through Monday forms; Monday sends the reminders, not people) and O-16, both this month. O-33 (link SOPs from Monday templates and retire stale pages) is a fill-in. This cause also shapes how every other fix is chosen: where people have already ignored a manual step, the shortlist automates it instead (Stage 2 lens note).
