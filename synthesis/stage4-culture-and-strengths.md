## Culture summary

Aperitif's culture is one of its strongest assets: across the interviews people describe a calm, warm, low-blame workplace where they are trusted to run their own month, judged on output rather than hours, and helped by colleagues when peaks hit. That trust shows in the work – pride in human-written content, senior review before anything reaches a client, advice given in the client's interest, and a team that arrives with its own ideas and welcomes change. The same warmth has a cost: leadership fears that pushing performance will make people leave, long relationships soften expectations, and agreed processes are treated as optional, so standards are reminded rather than enforced. Overload is absorbed quietly and personally, client-pleasing beats margin and scope, and knowledge and AI tools stay with individuals, which leaves the business fragile when someone is away or leaves. The aim is not to change the culture but to add the structure it lacks – clear standards, named owners and visible measures – so that calm and accountability can sit together.

### CP-01 · A calm, loyal culture makes standards and accountability hard to enforce

Leadership fears that pushing performance will make people quit; long personal relationships soften expectations; performance is judged by gut feel; peers avoid raising gaps for fear of blaming each other.

- Raised by: 3 staff + 2 leadership (KZ, TG, WS, KO, MP) · Reinforces: leadership-noted (attrition fear) + NEW · Severity: 4 · Confidence: High – raised by 3+ people
- Interviewer-sourced corroboration (not counted): B-K12
- "But now with this next generation, they just quit, man." – leadership, MP L313 ~27:44 (B-K14)
- "I don't want to throw the tech tape under the bus." – a senior SEO manager and link-building lead, KZ L316 ~32:57 (B-K22)
- Root cause: Pacing-sheet, SOP and pace standards are missed, and leadership falls back on reminders or a three-strikes rule (B-K04, B-K05). → Leaders hold back from firmer expectations because they fear attrition and long relationships make any change feel personal (B-K14, B-K06); peers avoid raising gaps so as not to blame each other (B-K22). → Performance is judged by feel; there are no visible role standards or output measures that make expectations impersonal (D-K11, B-K13, B-K29). → Behaviour/system: accountability rests on relationships, not on agreed, measured standards owned by team leads.
