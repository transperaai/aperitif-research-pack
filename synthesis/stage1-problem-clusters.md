## 1.2 Shared root causes – headline findings

Each cluster's why-chain ends at a system, process, tool or habit. Clusters that end at the same cause are grouped below; these eight causes are the pack's headline findings. Raise counts are unique people across the member clusters.

### RC-01 · No system of record: budgets, client data, briefs, links and files are held in many hand-maintained copies, so people re-key, reconcile and collate by hand.

**Drives:** C-01 (Paid budgets re-keyed by hand in three places, so pacing runs against wrong figures), C-05 (Managers spend hours collating data across platforms for calls, queries and analysis), C-06 (Client reports, reviews and decks are assembled and narrated by hand each cycle), C-20 (Content briefs are re-cut and reformatted by hand, eating the first half of each month), C-21 (Content hand-offs run on hand-copied sheets: briefs late or unseen, statuses double-keyed), C-23 (Link building runs on disconnected sheets; every order, link and invoice is re-keyed), C-34 (Client files and logins sit in personal drives and the CRM, not with the work)

**Evidence strength:** 12 staff + 2 leadership across 7 clusters (6 High confidence); max severity 5. **Anchor:** Operations capacity & process

### RC-02 · Monitoring is human: nothing pushes signals on spend, performance, tracking, link status, trends or churn, so problems surface late.

**Drives:** C-03 (Ad channels beyond Google and Meta have no pacing, so overspends go unseen), C-04 (Performance drops and tracking breaks surface only in manual checks, often at month-end), C-15 (Seasonal, trending and competitor signals are spotted too late, by hand), C-16 (Churn risk is spotted late and account teams report rather than advise), C-25 (Paid links vanish after payment and nobody re-checks that they stay live)

**Evidence strength:** 9 staff + 2 leadership across 5 clusters (4 High confidence); max severity 5. **Anchor:** Performance visibility & margin

### RC-03 · Client context isn't captured per client, so every hand-off – sales to delivery, manager to writer or specialist, cover – loses detail.

**Drives:** C-07 (Client knowledge sits in people's heads and personal docs, so briefs and cover suffer), C-08 (Client handovers (sales to delivery, offboarding) have no structured pack or trigger), C-12 (Regulated-industry content lacks shared compliance rules and credible sources), C-27 (Internal task briefs from managers to implementers are slow to write and often incomplete)

**Evidence strength:** 8 staff + 0 leadership across 4 clusters (3 High confidence); max severity 4. **Anchor:** Client experience & delivery

### RC-04 · Tools, automations and AI workflows are built by individuals with no owner, documentation, policy or renewal plan, so they break or stay personal.

**Drives:** C-02 (Pacing tools are broken or fragile, so Meta and Google pacing is done by hand), C-28 (Meta campaign builds are manual and every ad needs hand QA), C-31 (HubSpot–Make–Monday integrations create wrong or unverified tasks, at notable cost), C-32 (Home-built automations and tools have one builder, no documentation and no backup), C-33 (AI use is personal and ungoverned: no policy, shared skills, client context or seats)

**Evidence strength:** 11 staff + 1 leadership across 5 clusters (5 High confidence); max severity 5. **Anchor:** Technology, AI & stack

### RC-05 · There is no effort model: time per client isn't measured, scope and onboardings aren't checked against capacity, and intake isn't triaged – so work is sold, priced and scheduled by feel.

**Drives:** C-10 (Onboarding work is manual and heavy; clustered starts swamp BAU and wear staff down), C-17 (Managers are at capacity, so lower-tier clients and account hygiene slip first), C-18 (Unplanned same-day and high-touch client requests break the planned month), C-19 (Effort per client isn't tracked, so capacity, pricing and margin are set by feel), C-26 (Strategy and technical SEO run as separate queues, so big technical work slips), C-38 (Sales commits scope and spend that delivery can't meet profitably)

**Evidence strength:** 11 staff + 2 leadership across 6 clusters (6 High confidence); max severity 5. **Anchor:** Operations capacity & process

### RC-06 · Decisions and quality gates sit with a few senior people because authority isn't delegated and the management layer is thin.

**Drives:** C-22 (Content delivery hinges on one manager as editor, router and freelancer manager), C-24 (Link sourcing is slow, manual and supply-constrained, and AI vetting is inconsistent), C-36 (Escalations, sign-offs, QA and Tier 1 relationships funnel to a few senior people), C-37 (Management layer is thin: paid media lacks a senior lead; new leads lack training), C-39 (Growth depends on the founder and inbound leads while AI erodes core services)

**Evidence strength:** 8 staff + 2 leadership across 5 clusters (4 High confidence); max severity 4. **Anchor:** Leadership & dependency

### RC-07 · External dependencies – client access, approvals, inputs, contractors and vendors – aren't contracted, time-boxed or tracked, so delivery waits.

**Drives:** C-09 (Client onboarding stalls waiting on access, client inputs and the tracking contractor), C-11 (Content stalls at client approval with no client-facing approval trail), C-13 (Page builds and content uploads reach clients with errors or loop for weeks), C-14 (Client approval cycles hold up paid optimisations and launches, while managers carry the blame), C-29 (Creative and landing pages are outsourced, slow and hard to brief)

**Evidence strength:** 9 staff + 1 leadership across 5 clusters (5 High confidence); max severity 4. **Anchor:** Client experience & delivery

**Drives:** C-30 (Monday.com isn't the single home for work: requests bypass it and statuses go stale), C-35 (SOPs and new systems aren't adopted, so leaders re-explain and mistakes repeat)

**Evidence strength:** 5 staff + 2 leadership across 2 clusters (2 High confidence); max severity 4. **Anchor:** Leadership & dependency
