## 1. Five-line summary

1. **Onboarding is the #1 pain and a growth limit.** Month one is a strategist-built package (URL/keyword map, metadata, keyword list, 5–6 month content plan, link plan, E-E-A-T, competitors, strategy deck); a five-site month pushed Karl into out-of-hours work, and managers can't take several new clients at once. It was his magic-wand pick: "press the button and it's mostly done".
2. **Link QA doesn't exist.** Nobody checks that paid links are live, on the approved domain, or still there later; a one-off report found ~100 dead links over ~10 months (≈ $800–1,300 of paid links a month, estimated). The agency places 300–350 links a month.
3. **The link lead's time goes on admin.** Karl vets ~60-domain batches by hand (1–2 h each) and pays invoices; reviews wait "a couple of days", domains are added to the sheet unseen, and a link takes at least a month end to end.
4. **One client (Disney) drives a lot of manual work:** 5–10 localisation docs a week × 3 markets × 15–20 min, a bespoke three-market report (half a day+), Google Trends tracking only every 2–3 days, and missed event topics.
5. **Delivery handoffs leak quality.** Strategists have little say in technical priorities (simple tasks first; tech work sometimes unfinished at report time), and page builds reach clients with mobile errors because there's no structured build QA. Monthly briefs (end of month) and reports (week one) collide.
